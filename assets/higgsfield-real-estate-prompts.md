# Higgsfield Real Estate Video Prompt Templates

**Model:** `kling-v2-1-master` via Higgsfield's public API (`POST https://platform.higgsfield.ai/v1/image2video/kling`, body `params.model = "kling-v2-1-master"`) — Kling 3.0 Turbo was the original target but has no public API access as of 2026-08-11 (see revision history). `kling-v2-1-master` was chosen after live A/B testing against plain `kling-v2-1` and Seedance; confirmed via `params.model` enum validation these are the only two Kling variants Higgsfield's API currently exposes.
**Duration:** 5 seconds (Higgsfield API default for this model; not independently confirmed configurable)
**Resolution:** whatever `kling-v2-1-master` renders at by default — not an explicit request parameter on this endpoint (unlike Seedance, which does expose `resolution`)

Core formula: start zoomed in (or centered), move only within the boundaries of the original photo, explicitly block hallucinated objects, and lock straight vertical lines. This avoids the two most common failure modes — invented geometry (e.g. phantom doors) and flat "sliding picture" motion instead of real parallax.

**Revision history:**
- v2 (2026-08-11) — after reviewing actual generated clips (Higgsfield API, `kling-v2-1`/`kling-v2-1-master`), found two real failure modes the v1 prompts didn't prevent: (1) Zoom In/Out rendered as a flat digital crop-and-scale with zero parallax — confirmed by comparing start/mid/end frames, every object stayed in identical relative position, just enlarged uniformly, instead of a true 3D dolly where near objects shift/scale faster than far ones; (2) Pan Right/Left revealed entire invented areas (new doors, cabinets, furniture) beyond the source photo's edge, because a single flat photo has no ground truth past its own frame — asking the pan to "reveal more of the room" past that point forces the model to hallucinate. v2 fixes: explicit "not a flat zoom/crop, true 3D parallax required" language on the zoom moves, and a dialed-back pan distance + explicit no-invented-content escape hatch on the pan moves. Also added a shared negative prompt — Higgsfield's API has a real `negative_prompt` field, unused in v1.
- v3 (2026-08-11) — re-tested v2 on `kling-v2-1-master` by pulling actual frames from the results. v2's zoom fix technically produced real parallax, but the emphatic "true 3D dolly... vanishing point should shift" language pushed the model into visible corruption by the end of the clip (warped/duplicated architecture, glassy reflection artifacts, a hallucinated hallway bleeding through). v2's pan fix reduced but didn't eliminate hallucinated new decor/wall art past the frame edge. v3 softens the zoom language to a gentler, non-demanding parallax cue (removes the "vanishing point" instruction specifically, since that seems to be what triggered the worst artifacts) and shrinks the pan reveal to near-zero — treating the pan as a lateral drift within already-visible content rather than a "reveal" of new space at all. Negative prompt expanded to explicitly exclude reflections/mirrors/ghosting/duplicated architecture, which weren't called out in v2 and showed up as real artifacts. **Result: v3 zoom is clean (no artifacts, subtle real parallax, faithful room preservation) — accepted as final.** v3 pan is meaningfully better (no corruption) but still shows minor invented detail at the reveal edge (e.g. a small framed picture not in the source) — accepted as a reasonable trade-off given a single flat photo has no ground truth past its own edge; not expected to be fully eliminable via prompt wording alone on this model. **v3 is the final adopted prompt set — this is what n8n's `Build Clip Jobs` node should embed verbatim, alongside the negative prompt above and `model: "kling-v2-1-master"`.**

## Negative prompt (use for all four movements)

Pass this in Higgsfield's `negative_prompt` field alongside every request, regardless of movement:

extra people, human figures, pets, animals, invented furniture, new doorways, new rooms, new wall art, new decor, extra windows, warped architecture, duplicated columns, distorted geometry, morphing walls, glass reflections, mirror artifacts, ghosting, flat sliding image, static crop zoom, digital zoom artifact, watermark, text overlay, blurry, low quality artifacts

---

## 1. Zoom In / Push-In

Camera starts framing the center of the room at approximately 35% into the original photo, then performs a slow, smooth cinematic push-in, continuing to advance gently toward the center. The motion should have a natural sense of depth — foreground elements move and grow slightly more than elements further back, giving the push-in a subtle three-dimensional feel rather than a flat, static image zoom. Keep this effect subtle and natural, not exaggerated. Camera stays locked on-axis, moving forward only, no lateral movement. Preserve exact room contents, furniture, layout, lighting, and architecture from the source image — do not add, invent, or hallucinate any objects, doors, windows, reflections, or elements not visible in the original photo. Vertical lines on walls, windows, and door frames stay perfectly straight throughout. Realistic depth of field, sharp focus maintained on foreground and midground. Natural shadow behavior as the camera moves closer. Professional real estate videography quality, steady and clean, 5–6 seconds.

---

## 2. Pan Right

Camera starts zoomed in at approximately 35% of the original photo, positioned toward the left side of the room, then performs a very slow, subtle pan to the right — a gentle lateral drift within the space already shown in the original photo. The camera moves to the right throughout the shot, but the pan distance is minimal: the framing should stay almost entirely within content already fully visible in the source image, revealing at most a bare sliver of new space at the very right edge. Do not reveal an entire new area of the room, a new doorway, new furniture, or a new room. If any new space becomes visible at the edge, it must be minimal and visually consistent with the existing wall, floor, and ceiling — absolutely no new furniture, doors, cabinets, decor, artwork, or rooms that are not already visible in the original photo. Preserve exact room contents, furniture, layout, lighting, and architecture — do not add, invent, or hallucinate any objects, doors, windows, people, reflections, or elements not visible in the original photo. Vertical lines on walls, windows, and door frames stay perfectly straight throughout. Natural parallax, foreground elements shift slightly faster than the background. Realistic depth of field and shadow behavior. Professional real estate videography quality, steady and clean, 5–6 seconds.

## 4. Zoom Out / Pull-Back

Camera starts framing the center of the room at approximately 35% into the original photo, then performs a slow, smooth pull-back, gradually revealing the wider room until the final frame matches the full original source image exactly. The motion should have a natural sense of depth — foreground elements move and shrink slightly more than elements further back, giving the pull-back a subtle three-dimensional feel rather than a flat, static image zoom. Keep this effect subtle and natural, not exaggerated. Camera moves backward only, no lateral movement. The end framing must not extend beyond the boundaries of the original photo — no invented space, walls, or rooms beyond what the source image contains. Preserve exact room contents, furniture, layout, lighting, and architecture from the source image — do not add, invent, or hallucinate any objects, doors, windows, reflections, or elements not visible in the original photo. Vertical lines on walls, windows, and door frames stay perfectly straight throughout. Natural parallax, foreground elements shift slightly faster than the background as the camera pulls back. Realistic depth of field and shadow behavior. Professional real estate videography quality, steady and clean, 5–6 seconds.
